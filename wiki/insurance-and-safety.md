## Travel Insurance

Must cover:
- Hiking up to 3,300 m
- Via ferrata as a **named** activity (many SG plans exclude "mountaineering/climbing with ropes" — read the adventure-sports schedule)
- Search & rescue / helicopter ≥ S$50k
- Medical ≥ S$150k
- Trip cancellation

SG comparison notes: altitude caps vary hugely (FWD/MSIG ~3,000 m on base tiers; Income up to ~6,000 m; DirectAsia sells an "Extreme Sports" add-on). Budget ~S$120–260 pp for 2 weeks. Save the policy PDF and 24 hr hotline offline; screenshot certificates.

## Aiut Alpin Dolomites Membership

- €40 per person — local mountain-rescue cover for exactly these provinces (Bolzano/Belluno/Trento)
- Reimburses SAR/helicopter costs up to €75,000
- Cheap belt-and-braces cover on top of travel insurance
- Buy online at aiut-alpin-dolomites.com
- Real case cited in the source: a hiker who ignored a closed ferrata near Cortina was billed €14,225 for rescue

## Daily Weather Rhythm

The rule that matters most for Dolomites hiking in September: convective storms build **~14:00–16:00**.

- Trailhead by 07:30–08:30
- High point by 11:00–12:00
- Off summits/ridges/via ferratas by ~14:00
- Check Windy / Bergfex / 3Bmeteo + the relevant webcam every evening
- The flexible rest day (Sept 9–12, see [[open-todos]]) exists specifically to absorb a bad-weather day

## Health & Safety Summary

- **Altitude**: max sleeping altitude ~2,100 m (low AMS risk); day max 3,265 m (Marmolada, lift-assisted) — hydrate, take it easy on days 1–2, descend if headache + nausea worsens
- **Ticks** (TBE exists in Südtirol/Trentino): tuck trousers in, use repellent, do a daily tick check (groin/hairline/armpits), carry tweezers; avoid raw/unpasteurised milk and cheese from farms
- **Sun at altitude**: SPF50 every morning, sunglasses on even when cloudy
- **Storms**: the 14:00 rule above; on via ferrata, any thunder risk means don't start / turn back
- **Water**: valley tap water is safe; mountain fountains are seasonal; treat/filter hut tap water unless signed potabile
- **Medicines**: keep in original packaging + a doctor's letter; check Italian import rules for any controlled substances
